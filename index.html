Getting It Right

Why pricing right from day one matters

The most common mistake sellers make is starting high with the intention of coming down later. It's understandable — a neighbor sold for a strong number, or you have a target you need to hit. But the market doesn't know about that number, and it won't adjust to meet it.

Starting above market value almost always means a longer time on market, price reductions, and a lower final sale price than pricing correctly from the start. Buyers and their agents track price reductions closely — a home that sits and drops signals something's wrong, even when nothing is. You lose the most motivated buyers, the ones watching when you first went live.

What most sellers don't realize: buyers validate value before they ever schedule a showing. If a home looks overpriced, they simply move on — never call, never visit, and you never know they existed.

A well-priced home creates competition. Competition creates urgency. Urgency produces offers at or above asking. And the reverse holds too — every day a home sits on the market is another day that chips away at the credibility of its own price. The goal is to price where the market is today — not where it was, not where you hope it will be. This is the conversation we have before we set your price together.

Reading the Market

Understanding the market

Most sellers come to the table with a number in their head — usually from an online estimate or a neighbor's sale. Here's why that number is almost always wrong.

Lagging vs. leading indicators. Active inventory, sold prices, days on market — these are lagging indicators. Important, but by definition they describe the past. New listings and pending sales are leading indicators — they show where the market is heading before the lagging data catches up. In a changing market, these are the numbers that matter most.

National news isn't your market. Your home isn't in the national average — it's in a specific neighborhood, price range, and submarket. Even within King County, what's happening in Bellevue isn't what's happening in Sammamish; condos and single-family homes tell different stories at different price points.

Why online estimates fail sellers. They rely entirely on county records and lagging data — no knowledge of current momentum, no idea of your home's condition versus its comps. Pricing from an online estimate in a shifting market is like driving by looking in the rearview mirror.

At every appointment, I bring current leading and lagging data specific to your neighborhood and price range — so you know what the market is doing today, not three months ago.
